# Presenter notes — Drawing the Line

## 1. Opening / hook
Main idea: the boundary is not self-evident.
Say: people may place the line differently; my project asks how they justify that line.
Likely question: are you asking who is right? → No, I am asking how meanings are constructed.

## 2. Title
Main idea: state the project clearly.
Say: focus is social-media meaning-making around horse treatment at the World Nomad Games.
Likely question: why this title? → Because the project is about boundary-drawing.

## 3. Journey
Main idea: orient the audience quickly.
Say: I move from puzzle to literature to framework to positionality.
Likely question: none.

## 4. Puzzle
Main idea: one controversy may generate competing meanings.
Say: sport, risk, tradition, mistreatment, cruelty are possible framings; tradition may work in more than one direction.
Likely question: are these findings already? → No, they are possible meanings guiding the puzzle.

## 5. Research question + scope
Main idea: define the RQ and what interpret/negotiate mean.
Say: interpretation is assigning meaning; negotiation is interaction over meaning.
Likely question: do these users represent a wider population? → No, this is an interpretive, bounded discussion.

## 6. Why interpretivism?
Main idea: the question is about meaning, not prevalence.
Say: I am not measuring percentages or making a veterinary judgment; I am studying how participants themselves define the issue.
Likely question: are you saying harm is subjective? → No, physical harm may exist independently; that is simply not the research question.

## 7. Contribution
Main idea: the project adds a focused angle, not a novelty exaggeration.
Say: existing studies already show online welfare disagreement; I focus on how those judgments interact with tradition and proper horse treatment in this case.
Likely question: how is this different from Cheung et al.? → My case foregrounds tradition and cultural legitimacy.

## 8. Literature conversation 1
Main idea: welfare boundaries are socially contested.
Say: the literature shows ongoing dispute over where legitimate sport ends and cruelty begins.
Likely question: what does this literature establish? → That the boundary is contested.

## 9. Literature conversation 2
Main idea: care and use can coexist.
Say: horses may be understood through partnership and attentiveness while also being used within unequal human power relations.
Likely question: are sport and care opposites? → Not necessarily.

## 10. Literature conversation 3
Main idea: tradition is not one fixed thing.
Say: culturally meaningful horse relations can include multiple ideas of proper treatment.
Likely question: are you saying Central Asian tradition supports cruelty? → No. I am saying tradition itself can be interpreted in competing ways.

## 11. Where the conversations meet
Main idea: my study enters at the intersection of welfare, care, and tradition.
Say: I ask what happens when these layers come together in one controversy.
Likely question: why is this the gap? → Because these conversations are usually discussed separately.

## 12. Sensitising concepts
Main idea: explain the four concepts as lenses.
Say: they tell me where to look, not what I will find.
Likely question: are these coding categories? → Not fixed ones; they are sensitising concepts.

## 13. Meaning map
Main idea: show the analytical lens visually.
Say: acceptable horse treatment is the central question, and the four concepts help me notice how users define it.
Likely question: what if comments do not fit these concepts? → Then the framework should remain open and be revised.

## 14. Expectations
Main idea: expectations are provisional, not findings.
Say: tradition may work both ways; harm and cruelty may be separated; authority to judge may be contested.
Likely question: is this hypothesis testing? → No, these are interpretive expectations.

## 15. Positionality
Main idea: your standpoint offers both insight and risk.
Say: being from the region and initially concerned by the footage may shape what I notice; I need to remain open to alternative interpretations.
Likely question: how will you manage bias? → By actively looking for meanings that challenge my first reaction.

## 16. AI transparency
Main idea: show real collaboration without surrendering authorship.
Say: AI helped refine the question, framework, and literature organization; I still made final decisions and checked sources.
Likely question: what exactly did AI do? → See the listed tasks.

## 17. Reflection
Main idea: be honest about what became clearer and what remains unresolved.
Say: interpretation vs negotiation and sensitising concepts are clearer; sampling breadth and multilingual interpretation remain open.
Likely question: why keep unresolved issues? → Because this is still a research design in progress.

## 18. Conclusion
Main idea: return to the opening question with a stronger analytical answer.
Say: the study is not only about where I draw the line, but how participants themselves draw and justify it.
Likely question: what is the core contribution in one sentence? → Understanding boundary-making around horse treatment in a culturally symbolic sporting controversy.

## 19. References
Main idea: be transparent about the literature.
Say: separate academic sources from contextual sources.
Likely question: why include contextual sources? → They establish the case context, not the academic literature.
