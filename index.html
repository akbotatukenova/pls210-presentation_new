THE PUZZLE

One controversy, competing meanings

The same controversy may be framed as legitimate sport, acceptable risk, tradition, mistreatment, or cruelty.

ONE POSSIBILITY

Tradition may be invoked to defend the practice.

ANOTHER POSSIBILITY

Tradition may also be invoked to say that “real” tradition requires respect for horses.

Puzzle: how do participants construct and contest the boundaries between these meanings?

RESEARCH QUESTION

How do social-media users interpret and negotiate meanings of harm, cruelty, and tradition in discussions of horse treatment at the World Nomad Games?

INTERPRET an individual user assigns meaning to the horse treatment
NEGOTIATE users defend, challenge, qualify, or reframe those meanings through interaction
CASEVI World Nomad Games, Kyrgyzstan (2026): the bloodied-horse kokpar controversy
PLATFORMThreads, centered on the circulated post by Tilek Bekbolotov and its public discussion
POPULATIONusers participating in selected public comment threads and reply chains about the controversy
TIME7–14 September 2026 — a bounded analytical window around the controversy

Interpretive, not representative: the project does not claim these users stand for Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Central Asia, or social-media users generally.

WHY INTERPRETIVISM?

What kind of question is this?

I am not asking

“What percentage of users think this is cruel?”

I am asking

“What do cruelty, harm, sport, and tradition mean to the people discussing them?”

  • Meanings are contextual and can be contested.
  • I do not fix participants’ meanings completely in advance.
  • The researcher also interprets meaning, so reflexivity matters.
Studying interpretations of harm does not mean claiming physical suffering is unreal. It means veterinary assessment is not the question this study is designed to answer.

CONTRIBUTION

A focused extension

ALREADY IN THE LITERATURE

Horse welfare and legitimate sporting practice can be socially contested.

Cheung, Mills & Ventura (2026) already study social-media discourse around controversial horse treatment.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I examine how welfare judgments become intertwined with tradition, authenticity, cultural legitimacy, and ideas of proper human–horse relations in discussion of the World Nomad Games.

So the project extends existing scholarship — it does not claim “nobody has studied this.”

RETURN TO THE OPENING QUESTION

The line is itself the object of study.

The question is not only where I draw the line between sport, tradition, harm, and cruelty.

It is how people debating the same controversy draw that line for themselves — and what they invoke to justify it.

That boundary-making is what this research seeks to understand.
